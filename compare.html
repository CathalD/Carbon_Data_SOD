Comparisons

How the data differ and what can be compared. The McMaster/WWF maps (Sothe et al. 2022) come first.

Products are compared only where they report the same quantity at the same depth or pool. Each pair is compared on the coarser of its two grids, using 30,000 area-uniform random points (1,500 in the Hudson Plain for peat). Agreement is not accuracy: two maps can agree because they share training data. Full definitions are in Methods; statistics for every pair are in Downloads.

Maps

McMaster/WWF forest carbon vs SCANFI aboveground carbon

What and why. McMaster/WWF is the most cited Canadian forest carbon map; SCANFI is the national 30 m inventory-based biomass map. SCANFI biomass is converted to carbon (× 0.5 carbon fraction × 0.1, t/ha to kg/m²). The pools differ: McMaster/WWF includes belowground and dead carbon, so a higher McMaster/WWF value is expected.
Region · year · pool · grid. Canada · McMaster/WWF inputs mostly 2015–2019, SCANFI 2020 · total vegetation carbon vs aboveground carbon · McMaster/WWF ≈250 m grid (SCANFI averaged up).

McMaster/WWF is higher almost everywhere (a median 1.57× SCANFI aboveground carbon, interquartile range 0.96–2.69) and the two share their spatial pattern; the difference mixes the extra pools with genuine disagreement and cannot be split between them.

Mean A − B: 1.3 kg C/m² · RMSD: 2.97 kg C/m² · n: 10,616 points

Soils

Ground data

The five hosted profile datasets differ in where they sample, how deep they go and what they record.

Observations versus maps

Each profile takes the value of the map pixel that contains it, at the map’s native grid; observed values are thickness-weighted over layers that fully cover the map’s interval. A matching depth label is not enough, so profiles are matched by depth datum:

Sothe (McMaster/WWF) vs SoilGrids · SOC stock 0–100 cm

What and why. The McMaster/WWF soil carbon map against the global SoilGrids product, at the same 0–100 cm depth. SoilGrids does not release a 0–100 cm stock, so one is derived here by summing its carbon density over its five intervals; SoilGrids also excludes overlying organic layers.
Region · depth · grid. Canada · 0–100 cm · SoilGrids 250 m grid (Sothe averaged up).

Sothe is higher at 79% of points and the two maps are close to uncorrelated (r = 0.07). The largest gap is in the Hudson Plain (median 124 vs 22 kg C/m²), a peat-dominated region, where SoilGrids’ exclusion of overlying organic layers matters most.

Mean A − B: 27.1 kg C/m² · RMSD: 50.8 kg C/m² · n: 19,900 points

SOC stock 0–30 cm · SoilGrids vs NCSCDv2

What and why. Two released 0–30 cm stocks. SoilGrids excludes overlying organic layers, while NCSCDv2 is built from pedons measured from the soil surface, so read differences as indicative.
Region · depth · grid. Canada · 0–30 cm · NCSCDv2 0.012° grid (SoilGrids averaged up).

The two have similar averages but almost no shared pattern (r = -0.04; -0.03 without NCSCDv2’s 2.1% recorded zeros in rock and ice units).

Mean A − B: -1.17 kg C/m² · RMSD: 6.22 kg C/m² · n: 15,836 points

Peat thickness and peat carbon · Li et al. vs PEATGRIDS, Hudson Bay Lowlands

What and why. Two models of the full peat column. Thickness and whole-column carbon are separate quantities and are not ranked against fixed-depth stocks. PEATGRIDS missing values are masked before sampling.
Region · depth · grid. Hudson Plain · full modelled peat column · PEATGRIDS 1 km grid (Li 30 m averaged up).

Peat thickness agrees within ±25% at 67% of points (r = 0.63).

Mean A − B: -26.1 cm · RMSD: 47.9 cm · n: 1,230 points

Whole-column carbon does not agree (r = -0.02): Li’s carbon follows its peat depth almost exactly (r = 1.00), while PEATGRIDS carbon follows its thickness only weakly (r = 0.10).

Mean A − B: -37.6 kg C/m² · RMSD: 52.1 kg C/m² · n: 1,230 points

Soils

Profiles and layers first, then the covariates and training data behind each map, then the maps compared with each other and with the profiles.

1. Profile and layer data

Five prepared datasets hold 20,690 located profiles. They differ in where they sample, how deep they go and which variables they carry, and that shapes every comparison that follows.

These counts are not independent. WoSIS, the National Pedon Database and the peat compilations share profiles. Layers within a core are repeated depth observations, not separate locations. Profiles have not been deduplicated across datasets here.

3. Maps compared with each other

Only products with the same quantity and the same depth are paired. Each pair is compared on the coarser of its two native grids, with the finer product averaged up to it. Values are converted to one unit with the providers’ documented factors. Statistics come from 30,000 area-uniform random points across Canada (1,500 in the Hudson Plain for peat). Map figures are display-resolution summaries of the same differences.

Carbon stock 0–30 cm · SoilGrids vs NCSCDv2

The NCSCDv2 Canada rasterization covers nearly all land where SoilGrids has values, so the comparison spans most of the country. SoilGrids (250 m) is averaged to the NCSCDv2 0.012° grid.

Units: kg C/m². A minus B; RMSD = root-mean-square difference; r = Pearson correlation.
Comparison Points Mean A Mean B Mean A − B RMSD r Within ±25%
SOC stock 0–30 cm 15,836 6.72 7.89 -1.17 6.22 -0.04 20%

NCSCDv2 is exactly zero at 2.1% of points, mostly in the Pacific Maritime and Arctic Cordillera (rock and ice map units). These are recorded values and are kept; leaving them out gives r = -0.03, so the weak relationship is not caused by zeros. NCSCDv2 assigns stocks to soil-map polygons, while SoilGrids predicts each pixel, which is one reason their patterns differ.

Comparison Points Mean A Mean B Mean A − B RMSD r Within ±25%
SOC stock 0–100 cm (provisional) 15,804 49.6 20.5 29.1 48.6 0.28 10%

Across the shared area the median Sothe:NCSCDv2 ratio is 2.7 (interquartile range 1.4–5.3, points where NCSCDv2 is above zero). A ratio near 1 everywhere would support reading Sothe as 0–1 m kg C/m²; a consistently large ratio would point to a units or depth difference. The two maps also use different methods (pixel prediction versus soil-map polygons), so part of the gap is genuine disagreement. The profile comparison below adds field evidence.

Peat column · Li et al. vs PEATGRIDS, Hudson Bay Lowlands

Both model the full peat column, so peat thickness and whole-column carbon can be compared directly. Li (30 m) is averaged to the PEATGRIDS 1 km grid.

Units: cm (thickness); kg C/m² (carbon). A minus B; RMSD = root-mean-square difference; r = Pearson correlation.
Comparison Points Mean A Mean B Mean A − B RMSD r Within ±25%
Peat carbon (full column) 1,230 84.5 122 -37.6 52.1 -0.02 43%
Peat thickness 1,230 180 206 -26.1 47.9 0.63 67%

Thickness agrees far better than carbon. Within each product, Li’s whole-column carbon follows Li’s peat depth almost exactly (r = 1.00 across sampled pixels), whereas PEATGRIDS carbon barely follows PEATGRIDS thickness (r = 0.10). The two products build carbon from depth in different ways, so similar thickness does not give similar carbon.

4. Maps compared with profiles

Each profile takes the value of the map pixel that contains it, at the map’s native grid. Observed values are thickness-weighted over layers that fully cover the map’s interval. This is a comparison, not a validation: some datasets trained some maps (see above), and a 250 m pixel average is not the same support as a soil core.

Carbon stocks at profiles

For the Sothe units question: at profiles with an observed 0–100 cm stock, the ratio of Sothe’s median to the observed median is 1.5 for NPDB (126 profiles) and 1.2 for NRCan peat (983 profiles). That is much closer than its ratio to NCSCDv2, which suggests that NCSCDv2, not only Sothe, contributes to the map-to-map gap. These profiles are few and peat-heavy, so this narrows the question but does not settle it.

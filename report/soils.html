Soils

Profiles and layers first, then the covariates and training data behind each map, then the maps compared with each other and with the profiles.

1. Profile and layer data

Five prepared datasets hold 20,690 profile records, of which 1,116 fall outside Canada’s terrestrial ecozones; they are not deduplicated. The datasets differ in where they sample, how deep they go and which variables they carry, and that shapes every comparison that follows.

These counts are not independent. WoSIS, the National Pedon Database and the peat compilations share profiles. Layers within a core are repeated depth observations, not separate locations. Profiles have not been deduplicated across datasets here.

3. Maps compared with each other

Products are paired only when quantity and depth or pool match. The McMaster/WWF (Sothe et al.) comparisons come first because they are the most cited Canadian carbon maps. Each pair is compared on the coarser of its two native grids, with the finer product averaged up to it. Values are converted with the providers’ documented factors. Statistics and maps both come from 30,000 area-uniform random points (1,500 in the Hudson Plain for peat), and each band is sampled independently, so a point missing one depth is dropped only for that pair.

Sothe (McMaster/WWF) vs SoilGrids · SOC stock 0–100 cm

Same nominal depth, two caveats kept visible. Sothe’s hosted v2.0 stock is used as stored; its archive identifies the top 1 m, but the hosted file’s identity and datum are not yet verified. SoilGrids does not release a 0–100 cm stock, so one is derived here by summing its carbon density over its five intervals to 1 m; SoilGrids also excludes overlying organic layers. Sothe (≈250 m in degrees) is averaged to the SoilGrids 250 m grid.

Units: kg C/m². A minus B; RMSD = root-mean-square difference; r = Pearson correlation.
Comparison Points Mean A Mean B Mean A − B RMSD r Within ±25%
SOC stock 0–100 cm 19,900 48.1 20.9 27.1 50.8 0.07 19%

The two maps are close to uncorrelated nationally (Spearman ρ = 0.13), and within ecozones ρ ranges from -0.05 to 0.53. The largest gap is in the Hudson Plain, where the median Sothe stock is 124 kg C/m² against 22 for SoilGrids. That is a peat-dominated region, where organic-layer treatment matters most.

Before reading these differences as disagreement about soil carbon, check the depth datum and organic-layer treatment: SoilGrids removes overlying organic layers, and the treatment in Sothe’s hosted file is not yet confirmed. The difference warrants checking source identity, depth definition and processing; agreement alone cannot verify units or depth.

4. Maps compared with profiles

Each profile takes the value of the map pixel that contains it, at the map’s native grid. Observed values are thickness-weighted over layers that fully cover the map’s interval. This is a comparison, not a validation: some datasets trained some maps (see above), and a pixel average is not the same support as a soil core.

Eligibility. A matching depth label is not enough; the depth datum must match the product too:

Map products Profile depth datum Profiles used Why
Geng, SoilGrids (SOC, BD, 0–30 cm stock, derived 0–100 cm stock) Mineral surface: organic surface layers removed, first mineral layer = 0 cm WoSIS, NPDB Mineral-soil products; SoilGrids removes overlying organic layers. Peat, coastal and Riley profiles are outside these products’ scope
Sothe, NCSCDv2 (stocks) Profile surface, organic layers included All datasets with stocks Organic-inclusive stock products (Sothe’s datum still to verify)
PEATGRIDS, Li et al. (peat thickness) Recorded peat (organic) depth per core NRCan peat Full-column peat products
